Why now

The Supreme Court opened the door. Your process is what's on the other side.

In Montgomery v. Caribe Transport, the Supreme Court held that federal law does not categorically block state-law claims that a freight broker negligently selected an unsafe carrier. The Court did not hand brokers a checklist — it opened the door for a broker's carrier-selection process to be examined under state negligence law.

That changes what "vetting" means. Having checked a carrier and being able to show it are different things. You may need to show what you checked, when you checked it, what you found, and why you decided — for the specific load, at the moment you committed.

Questions

Fair questions, straight answers.

Does CarrierBasis replace Highway, Carrier411, or my other data tools?

No. Those services provide information about carriers. CarrierBasis applies your policy to the information you have, guides the review, and preserves the decision as a sealed, load-specific record. Most brokerages keep their existing data subscriptions.

Is this legal advice? Does it guarantee protection from liability?

No. CarrierBasis is software, not counsel, and no process eliminates the risk of being sued. What it does is make your carrier-selection process consistent, complete, and preserved — organized around the safety factors courts are examining in broker negligent-selection cases.

What does "sealed" mean?

Sealed means locked at approval and fingerprinted with a SHA-256 hash. Any later change to the record's contents would no longer match the fingerprint — so the record can be verified as unchanged since commitment.

Who is recorded as the decision-maker?

The logged-in reviewer who approved the record — a named, authenticated user, not a shared login or a blank field. Their written basis for the decision is part of the sealed record.

What if a carrier has a problem in its history?

An isolated concern does not automatically disqualify a carrier — but it should be reviewed in context and resolved on the record. CarrierBasis is built for real carriers with real histories: it documents the judgment rather than hiding everything behind a green light.

How long does a vetting take?

Facts are pulled and evaluated automatically — FMCSA authority, safety and inspection history, your TMS data, submitted documents — so the reviewer's time goes to judgment, not data gathering. Routine approvals take a few minutes. Reviews involving material concerns take longer — because the reviewer is exercising judgment, not clearing another screen.
